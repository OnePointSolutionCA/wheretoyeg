      A Disappointing Experience at a New Asian Indian Restaurant -
      My husband and I were genuinely excited to try this Asian Indian restaurant. The ambience was inviting, the seating was comfortable, and the spread at the buffet looked promising — from gol gappe and chaat to a variety of curries and desserts. Our hopes were high.
      
      Sadly, what followed was one of the most disappointing dining experiences we've had in a long time.
      
      We worked our way through the buffet — goat curry, butter chicken, mushroom jalfrezi, veg pasta, and Amritsari fish — and each dish fell flat in its own way. The curries tasted as though boiled chicken and mutton had simply been dunked into a flavorless gravy. There was no depth, no warmth, no soul to the cooking. The Amritsari fish, a dish that should be crispy and golden, had batter that was completely raw inside. The pasta was bland. The mango custard was tasteless. The moong dal halwa — a dessert that should be rich and fragrant — tasted stale. Even the garlic naan missed the mark. Butter milk and golgappe were good!
      
      I want to be clear: I am someone who genuinely loves sharing positive experiences, and it takes a lot for me to write a critical review. But this felt important to share, especially for a restaurant still finding its footing.
      
      To the management's credit, they graciously waived the buffet charges for one person — a kind gesture that did not go unnoticed.
      This isn't a complaint — it's heartfelt feedback, and I'm sharing it because I want this restaurant to succeed: 1. Revisit every recipe from scratch. Indian cuisine is built on layers of spice, technique, and love — the current versions don't reflect that.
      
      2. The owner should personally visit well-established Indian restaurants in the city to taste what authentic Indian food feels and tastes like.
      
      I'm attaching photos to give a clearer picture. I hope the team takes this in the spirit it's intended — as an opportunity to grow and truly do justice to a cuisine that deserves so much better.
