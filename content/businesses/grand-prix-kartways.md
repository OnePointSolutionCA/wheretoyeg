      We recently visited Splattersverse at West Edmonton Mall as a family of four, and unfortunately, it was one of the most disappointing experiences of our trip.
      We paid approximately $35 per person for the basic experience and arrived 15 minutes before our scheduled 3:00 p.m. time slot.
      
      After checking in, we were directed to wait in the arcade until an employee came to escort us to what appeared to be a maintenance/storage room to change into ponchos and shoe covers.
      
      After a brief explanation, we were left on our own in the paint room. One of the first things we noticed was that there were no tables, chairs, easels, or any surfaces on which to place our canvases while painting. As a family hoping to create a meaningful keepsake together, this made it awkward and difficult to create our artwork.
      Despite that, we took time creating individual designs on our canvases with the provided acrylic paint, hoping to combine them into a special family piece. However, when we moved on to the "glow paint," we quickly discovered that it was so watered down that it completely washed away the artwork we had already created.
      
      The glow paint behaved more like coloured water than paint. Because the paint was so watery, the interactive portion of the experience lasted less than 10 minutes before there was essentially nothing left to splatter. The paint simply rolled off our ponchos instead of sticking to them. There was also no real "glow effect" afterward because the canvases, ponchos, and everything else had been washed out by the watery paint.
      
      In fact, our entire family had purchased white Converse sneakers specifically for this experience, hoping to leave with fun, paint-splattered souvenirs. Instead, our shoes looked almost brand new when we left.
      When the experience ended, we let ourselves out and returned to collect our belongings.
      
      Unfortunately, the staff interaction was equally disappointing. Staff members appeared disengaged, with no smiles or acknowledgment of our presence. When I asked whether it was normal for the paint to be so watery and not adhere to anything, two staff members laughed and commented to each other that the colleague who mixed our paint had essentially made "straight-up water." I then asked if I could quickly wash my hands in the sink in the room and was told no.
      
      As we walked ourselves out, we could still hear staff discussing how watery the paint had been for our session.
      
      Beyond the poor quality of the materials, the overall atmosphere was surprisingly dull. There was no music in the paint room, little staff engagement, and no energy or excitement to enhance the experience. From start to finish, our actual painting experience lasted approximately 15 minutes.
      
      For the price, this experience was extremely disappointing and felt like a complete waste of money. If you're visiting West Edmonton Mall, I would strongly recommend skipping Splattersverse and spending your time and money exploring one of the many other attractions instead.
