      We turned up for our round just as they opened on a Thursday and were the first ones to play.
      
      The course superintendent at the front desk provided us each a club. I insisted on returning the one I was given for a longer one.  My performance would have suffered with the putter I was originally provided which the superintendent should have deduced from my height.  I got the distinct impression he was not fully invested in my success on the course.
      
      We at first were unable to locate the cup on hole #1. We then realized we were in the mirror maze. Once we reached the actual first tee my children began the round, followed by my wife, and then myself.
      
      While my wife is best known for hitting her ball out of play, suprisingly she took the early lead with a birdie on #1 as we all made par.
      
      After the first three holes my wife and I were tied at -1, while double bogeys for both my tween and teen children put them 3 off the lead.
      
      Over the rest of the front nine I began to showcase my putting prowess and dominated my wife and children. At the turn my -6 score far eclipsed my wife at +1, and my children at +4 and +6.
      
      Hole #10 is where things got interesting. My wife's errant putt left the playing surface and landed on the hole behind us where another grouping was in play.  I scolded my wife for her poor etiquette and apologized to the grouping behind us for breaking their concentration. Although their 5-year-old was able to recover from the shock and make birdie.  Fortunately the course marshall was not present to issue her a penalty or remove her from the course.
      
      My brilliance was on display with my third straight birdie, in what would grow to become a string of eight consecutive birdies.
      
      The back nine proved no match for our skills. Everyone in our grouping  racked up birdie after birdie, and an eagle for my tween child on #14 to pull ahead of her sibling.
      
      Unfortunately the pressure got to her  and she triple bogeyed #16 after several failed attempts to get her ball in the elevator.  Bogeys on #17 and #18 brought her back to earth to finish in a tie at +6 with my teen.
      
      #16 was the only hole on the back nine I did not birdie on my way to shooting an incredible -14 round of 39.  The superintendemt was unable to tell my if it was a course record. But it was an incredible round that left my wife and children far out of contention.
      
      The sound effects and artwork were enjoyable. Although the course design was a bit monotonous and not challenging for my amazing skill level.
      
      The greens fees were high for what the course offered, especially considering the putter I was originally provided from the pro shop.
      
      OK overall.
