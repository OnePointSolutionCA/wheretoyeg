      I joined 30 Minute Hit on the south side of Edmonton after trying a couple classes and honestly feeling really good about it at first. The environment felt positive, motivating, and empowering, and I was excited to continue.
      
      But during my third session...after signing a contract... I experienced intense emotional overwhelm while training. I was trying to breathe through it and regulate myself, but instead of recognizing that I was struggling, I kept being told to “push through.”
      
      After reading the studio’s response to my original review, I feel the need to clarify some things because parts of it did not reflect my actual experience.
      
      Kim was on her way out the door because she was late for an appointment, and Brandy did not meaningfully check in with me. At one point, while I was visibly trying not to completely fall apart, struggling to regulate myself, she said, “I know it’s hot in here, honey,” before walking away from the interaction entirely.
      
      That comment felt incredibly dismissive and condescending considering what was actually happening.
      
      There also were not multiple women on the floor needing attention at the time...it was only myself and my friend.
      
      My point has never been that anyone was intentionally trying to be harmful. My point is that in a women-centered self-defense environment, trauma-informed awareness should matter more than this.
      
      I honestly do not know a single woman who has not experienced some form of trauma, and high-intensity environments that involve adrenaline, aggression, pressure, and being pushed physically can absolutely trigger emotional responses in people unexpectedly.
      
      There is a huge difference between motivating someone through physical discomfort and recognizing when someone’s nervous system is overwhelmed. Being repeatedly told to “push through” while visibly distressed did not feel supportive to me. It felt emotionally unsafe.
      
      I also want to mention that when I came in to cancel my membership afterward, nobody asked why I was leaving. Brandy simply pointed to where I needed to sign and date the paperwork. While reading through the cancellation form myself, I found the section asking why I was cancelling and filled it out on my own.
      
      At no point did anyone ask what happened, show concern, or act like they cared why I no longer felt comfortable continuing there. The interaction felt transactional more than compassionate, which honestly just reinforced how I already felt after the training experience.
      
      I am sharing this because I think women-centered fitness and self-defense spaces should take trauma-informed awareness more seriously. Intent matters, but impact matters too.
