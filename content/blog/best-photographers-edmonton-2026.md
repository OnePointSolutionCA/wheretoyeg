Everyone with a camera calls themselves a photographer. The difference between a good one and a cousin-with-a-DSLR shows up in three places: consistency across lighting conditions, turnaround time, and whether they deliver what they promised.

Edmonton has hundreds of photographers. Here's who's worth the investment for the work that matters.

## Wedding photography

Your wedding photos are the only record of the day. This is not where you save money.

**Lauren Hannah Photography** shoots natural light and candid moments. Her editing style leans warm and film-like without the heavy orange-and-teal filter that ages badly. She books out 8–12 months in advance for summer weddings.

**Carlos Vicente Photography** does both photo and video, which simplifies coordination. His work is editorial-leaning — posed shots that don't look posed. Good for couples who want magazine-quality portraits alongside candid reception shots.

**Admire Studios** handles larger weddings well. If you have 200+ guests and need a team (not a solo shooter), they scale without quality dropping.

**Dainty Develops Photography** is newer but building a strong portfolio. More affordable than the established names, and the work holds up. Good option if your budget is tight but you still want professional results.

### What wedding photography costs in Edmonton

- **Budget ($1,500–2,500):** Solo shooter, 4–6 hours, digital delivery only. Fine for small weddings and elopements.
- **Mid-range ($3,000–5,000):** Lead + second shooter, 8–10 hours, online gallery, print rights. Where most couples land.
- **Premium ($5,000–10,000+):** Full team, engagement session included, album design, same-day edits.

## Portraits and headshots

**Setph Studio** does clean headshots for professionals. LinkedIn-ready, corporate-appropriate, and they have studio lighting dialled in so every shot is consistent. Good for teams that need 10–20 headshots in one session.

**Ryan Parker Photography** works across portrait styles — from creative editorial to straightforward business headshots. Flexible on location.

**Maverick Studios** handles both individual and group portrait sessions. They're efficient with time, which matters when you're paying by the hour.

## Real estate photography

If you're selling a home or listing a rental, professional photos aren't optional anymore. Listings with professional photography sell faster — this is documented, not opinion.

**QuikSell Real Estate Photography** is built for speed. They shoot, edit, and deliver within 24 hours. Virtual tours, drone shots, and floor plans available. Most realtors in Edmonton use them or someone like them.

**Proper Exposure** does high-end real estate work — luxury listings, architectural photography, and twilight shots. More expensive, but the work justifies it for $500K+ listings.

## How to hire a photographer

- **Book early.** Summer weekends in Edmonton book out 6–12 months ahead for weddings. Headshot sessions need 2–3 weeks lead time.
- **Ask to see full galleries, not just highlights.** Anyone can pick 10 great photos. The question is whether the other 400 are also usable.
- **Get the contract in writing.** Number of edited images, turnaround time, print rights, and cancellation policy. If they won't put it in writing, walk.
- **Ask about backup equipment.** For weddings especially — a photographer with one camera body and no backup is a risk you don't need.
