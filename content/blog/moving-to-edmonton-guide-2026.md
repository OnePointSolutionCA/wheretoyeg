People move to Edmonton for the jobs, the housing prices, and the space. They stay because the city grows on you, slowly, then all at once. If you're planning a move in 2026, here's what the relocation brochures won't tell you.

## The weather, let's get this out of the way

Yes, it gets cold. January averages hover around -15°C and dip to -30°C or worse a few times each winter. You need a block heater, a good winter coat, and the understanding that your car will make sounds in January that would alarm you in July.

But Edmonton also gets more sunshine than Toronto. Summer days stretch past 10 PM. The river valley is one of the largest urban park systems in North America. Spring and fall are short but gorgeous.

**What to buy before your first winter:**
- Block heater and timer (set it for 2 hours before you leave)
- Winter tires: not all-seasons, actual winter tires
- A parka rated to at least -30°C
- Insulated boots with grip
- A plug-in extension cord for your parking stall

## Neighborhoods, where to live

### Affordable and family-friendly

**Mill Woods:** Southeast Edmonton. Diverse, affordable, good schools. Strip malls with some of the best ethnic food in the city. Transit access to the LRT is improving.

**Castle Downs:** North Edmonton. Quiet, residential, established. Good for families who want space and don't need to be close to downtown.

**Beverly / Abbottsfield:** Northeast. Most affordable entry point for homebuyers. Changing fast. Close to the Yellowhead corridor for commuters.

### Mid-range and popular

**Windermere / Heritage Valley:** South Edmonton. New builds, young families, big-box retail. Everything is clean and new but you're driving everywhere.

**Sherwood Park:** Technically Strathcona County, not Edmonton. Suburban, safe, good schools. 20-minute commute to downtown on a clear day.

**St. Albert:** North of Edmonton. Its own city with its own character. Great farmers market. Slightly higher property taxes but a noticeably different feel from Edmonton proper.

### Central and walkable

**Downtown / Oliver:** High-rises, condos, walkable to restaurants and the river valley. Parking is expensive. Nightlife is close. Best transit access in the city.

**Whyte Ave / Old Strathcona:** Edmonton's most walkable neighborhood. Independent shops, live music, restaurants. Rents are higher but you can actually live without a car here.

## Cost of living

Edmonton is still one of the most affordable major cities in Canada. No provincial sales tax in Alberta. You only pay the 5% federal GST.

**2026 rough numbers:**
- **1-bedroom apartment (central):** $1,200-$1,600/month
- **1-bedroom apartment (suburbs):** $900-$1,200/month
- **3-bedroom house (purchase):** $350,000-$550,000
- **Groceries (couple, monthly):** $500-$700
- **Car insurance:** $120-$200/month
- **Utilities (house, winter):** $250-$400/month

Gas is cheaper than Vancouver or Toronto. Property taxes are moderate. The big variable is heating, budget more for utilities in winter.

## Getting around

Edmonton is a car city. The LRT runs three lines (Capital, Metro, and Valley), but coverage is still limited compared to Calgary or Vancouver. Bus service exists but runs thin outside peak hours in suburban areas.

**If you're driving:** The Henday (Anthony Henday Drive) is the ring road. Learn it. Whitemud and Yellowhead are the east-west arteries. Winter driving is a skill you'll develop whether you want to or not.

**If you're not driving:** Live near an LRT stop (downtown, University, NAIT, Century Park, Mill Woods). Whyte Ave and downtown are the most walkable areas. E-scooters and bike lanes are expanding but winter limits their usefulness to about 6 months.

## Setting up your life

### Internet and phone
Telus and Shaw (now Rogers) are the big two. Telus Fibre is widely available and generally the fastest option. Expect $80-$120/month for a decent internet package.

### Healthcare
Register for the Alberta Health Care Insurance Plan as soon as you arrive. If you're coming from another province, your old provincial coverage carries you for up to three months while Alberta coverage starts. Walk-in clinics are plentiful, find them on our [medical page](/medical). Family doctors have waitlists, so get on one immediately.

### Banking
All major banks have branches everywhere. If you're new to Canada, RBC, TD, and BMO have newcomer banking packages.

## The things nobody tells you

- **Edmonton has incredible food.** Seriously. The halal food scene, the Vietnamese strip on 97 Street, the bakeries in Mill Woods: it punches way above its weight.
- **The river valley is massive.** 160 km of trails. You can mountain bike, cross-country ski, and hike without leaving the city.
- **Festival City is a real nickname.** Fringe, Folk Fest, Heritage Days, K-Days, Ice on Whyte. There's something almost every weekend in summer.
- **Winters are long but manageable.** Block heater, warm layers, and accepting that you'll idle your car for 10 minutes in January. Everyone does it.
- **People are genuinely friendly.** This isn't marketing copy. Edmontonians are disarmingly nice and it takes newcomers a while to stop being suspicious about it.
