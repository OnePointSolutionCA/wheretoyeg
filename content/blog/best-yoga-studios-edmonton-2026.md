Yoga in Edmonton ranges from sweaty 40-degree hot rooms to gentle stretch classes where you might fall asleep. Both are valid. The trick is picking the right studio for what you actually want.

## Hot yoga

Hot yoga means the room is heated to 35–40°C. You will sweat through your clothes in the first 15 minutes. It's not dangerous if you stay hydrated, but it's not a casual workout either.

**Metta Hot Yoga** is the most popular hot yoga studio in Edmonton. Their heated vinyasa classes are packed — book early for evening slots. The instructors push you without being aggressive about it. Bring two towels.

**YEG Cycle** combines cycling and yoga — if you want the intensity of a spin class and the recovery of yoga in the same membership, this covers both.

## Vinyasa and flow

Vinyasa is the "workout" style of yoga. You're moving continuously, linking poses with breath. It builds strength and flexibility, and a good class leaves you genuinely tired.

**Above Average Yoga and Wellness** runs strong vinyasa classes. The instructors cue well — they tell you where to put your hands and feet instead of assuming you know. Good for intermediate practitioners who want to be challenged.

**Flo n Glo Yoga Studio** leans more creative with their flows. If you're bored of the same sun-salutation sequence every class, try their signature flows. They also run candlelight sessions on Friday evenings.

## Yin and restorative

Yin yoga is slow. You hold poses for 3–5 minutes. It targets connective tissue, not muscles. It looks easy and it's not — sitting in a deep hip opener for four minutes while your brain screams at you to move is its own kind of hard.

**Atma Yoga Edmonton** runs dedicated yin classes that are properly paced. The room isn't heated, the lights are low, and the instructor talks you through the discomfort instead of ignoring it.

## Best for beginners

If you've never done yoga, showing up to an advanced vinyasa class and trying to keep up is a miserable experience. Start with a beginner series.

**Above Average Yoga** runs intro workshops that teach the fundamental poses before throwing you into a flow. Two sessions and you'll know enough to follow a regular class.

**Flo n Glo** offers beginner-specific classes on their schedule — not mixed-level classes that claim to accommodate beginners but actually move too fast for them.

## What to know before your first class

- **Arrive 10 minutes early.** You need to check in, stash your stuff, and find a spot. Walking in late disrupts everyone.
- **Bring your own mat.** Studio rentals work in a pinch, but they're shared and not always clean. A basic mat costs $25 at any sporting goods store.
- **Wear fitted clothes.** Loose shirts fall over your head in downward dog. Not a safety issue, just annoying.
- **Eat light beforehand.** A full stomach and inversions don't mix. Eat 2 hours before class, or have a small snack 30 minutes before.
- **Hot yoga hydration:** Drink water all day before class, not just right before. You lose more fluid than you think.

## Pricing

Edmonton yoga studios typically run:

- **Drop-in:** $18–25 per class
- **Monthly unlimited:** $100–160
- **Intro offers:** Most studios sell a 2-week unlimited pass for $30–40 for first-timers. Try 3–4 studios before committing to a membership.
