## Bouldering vs. rope climbing — what's the difference?

**Bouldering** is climbing short walls (12–15 feet) without ropes. You fall onto thick crash pads. Problems (routes) are graded V0–V16. Most beginners start at V0–V2.

**Top rope** climbing uses a rope anchored at the top of a tall wall (30–50 feet). A belayer manages the rope while you climb. You need a harness, shoes, and a belay partner.

**Lead climbing** is where you clip the rope into anchors as you go up. More advanced, requires certification at most gyms.

If you've never climbed before, bouldering is the easiest entry point — show up, rent shoes, start climbing.

## What to expect your first time

- **Cost:** $15–22 for a day pass, $5–8 for shoe rental
- **What to wear:** Athletic clothes that let you move. Avoid jeans. Climbing shoes are rented at the gym.
- **How long:** Most people spend 1.5–2 hours their first session. Your forearms will tell you when to stop.
- **Do I need to be fit?** No. Climbing is about technique and balance, not brute strength. Kids and seniors climb.

## Indoor climbing in Edmonton — winter training

Edmonton's climbing season outdoors is May–September. The river valley has some spots, but most local climbers train indoors year-round and travel to the Rockies for outdoor sends. A gym membership ($55–80/month) is cheaper than a ski pass and arguably a better workout.

## Wall climbing events and community

Most gyms run weekly bouldering nights, competitions, and social climbs. Follow their socials for event schedules. The Edmonton climbing community is welcoming — don't be afraid to ask someone for beta (advice) on a problem.
