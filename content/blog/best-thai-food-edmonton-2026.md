Edmonton's Thai food scene covers everything from classic pad thai joints to restaurants doing proper Isaan-style dishes that'll light your mouth up. Whether you want the comforting sweetness of massaman curry or the sour punch of real tom yum, the city has you covered.

## The classics

[Siam Thai Kitchen](/restaurants/siam-thai-kitchen) is one of Edmonton's most trusted Thai spots. Their green curry is rich and fragrant, and the pad thai hits that perfect balance of sweet, sour, and salty. They also have a second location on [St. Albert Trail](/restaurants/siam-thai-kitchen-st-albert-trail) for north-siders.

[Thai Avenue Restaurant](/restaurants/thai-avenue-restaurant) delivers consistent, well-executed Thai staples. The mango sticky rice is a must-order for dessert. Their curries come with the right amount of heat — enough to warm you up, not enough to ruin your evening.

[Thai Boat](/restaurants/thai-boat) is a long-standing favorite. The name is simple, the food is solid, and if you're in the area you already know. Their tom kha gai (coconut chicken soup) is one of the better versions in the city.

## For adventurous eaters

[Numchok Wilai Restaurant](/restaurants/numchok-wilai-restaurant) is where you go when you want Thai food that goes beyond the North American greatest hits. They serve dishes with real heat and authentic flavors. If you see something on the menu you don't recognize, order it.

[Zaab Thai - YEG](/restaurants/zaab-thai---yeg) brings Isaan (northeastern Thai) flavors. "Zaab" means delicious in Isaan dialect, and the food lives up to it. Think som tum (papaya salad) with proper funk, larb with toasted rice powder, and grilled meats with sticky rice.

[LOFT Thai Eatery](/restaurants/loft-thai-eatery) offers a more modern take on Thai dining. Clean presentation, solid technique, and a menu that balances familiar and adventurous. A good date night pick.

## Hidden gems

[Thai Corner Restaurant & Bakery](/restaurants/thai-corner-restaurant-bakery) does double duty — Thai food and baked goods. It's an unusual combo that works. The curries are legit, and you can grab pastries on your way out.

[Thai Valley Grill](/restaurants/thai-valley-grill) is a neighborhood spot that doesn't get as much attention as the bigger names but delivers quality Thai cooking at fair prices.

[Boualouang Laos & Thai Cuisine](/restaurants/boualouang-laos-thai-cuisine) blends Lao and Thai cooking. Laotian food shares roots with Isaan Thai, so you get dishes like laab and sticky rice alongside Thai curries. If you've never tried Lao food, this is your entry point.

## What to order if you're new to Thai food

- **Pad thai** — stir-fried rice noodles with tamarind sauce, peanuts, bean sprouts, and your choice of protein. The dish that got most people into Thai food.
- **Green curry (gaeng keow wan)** — coconut milk curry with Thai basil, bamboo shoots, and green chilies. Creamy, fragrant, and just spicy enough.
- **Tom yum** — hot and sour soup with lemongrass, galangal, lime leaves, and chilies. The shrimp version (tom yum goong) is the classic.
- **Mango sticky rice** — warm sticky rice with fresh mango and coconut cream. Simple, perfect, and the best dessert on any Thai menu.

## The heat question

Most Edmonton Thai restaurants ask your spice level on a 1-to-5 scale. A 3 is "Thai medium" and will have real heat. If you want it authentic, ask for Thai spicy — just know what you're signing up for. No shame in ordering mild either. The flavors are still all there.

Edmonton's Thai restaurants punch above their weight. Skip the chains, hit these local spots, and eat well.
