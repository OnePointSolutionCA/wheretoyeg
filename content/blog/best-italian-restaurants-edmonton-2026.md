Edmonton's Italian food scene ranges from old-school red-sauce joints to modern trattorias doing handmade pasta with local ingredients. Whether you want a casual weeknight plate of spaghetti or a proper multi-course Italian dinner, the city delivers.

## Fine dining and special occasions

[ACERO, Artisanal Cuisine](/restaurants/acero-artisanal-cuisine) is one of Edmonton's best restaurants, period. Modern Italian cooking with precision — handmade pasta, seasonal ingredients, and a wine list that'll keep you browsing. This is where you go for anniversaries, promotions, or any night you want to eat really well.

[Sorrentino's St. Albert](/restaurants/sorrentinos-st-albert) is an Edmonton institution. The Sorrentino's name has been part of the city's dining scene for years, and the St. Albert location keeps the standard high. Classic Italian dishes done with care, a strong wine program, and the kind of service that makes a regular Tuesday feel special.

## Trattoria style

[Vaticano Cucina](/restaurants/vaticano-cucina) brings a warm, inviting take on Italian cooking. Think rustic pastas, braised meats, and the kind of dishes Italian grandmothers actually make. Unpretentious and delicious.

[Porta Romana Restaurant](/restaurants/porta-romana-restaurant) is a classic. Named after the historic gate in Florence, it serves traditional Italian dishes with proper technique. Their osso buco and veal dishes are standouts.

[Cafe Amore Bistro](/restaurants/cafe-amore-bistro) is cozy and romantic — the name says it all. A good pick for date night when you want Italian food in an intimate setting without the fine-dining price tag.

[Cafe Celeste Bistro](/restaurants/cafe-celeste-bistro) delivers a similar vibe with its own personality. Fresh pasta, good sauces, and a neighborhood feel that keeps locals coming back.

## Modern Italian

[Olia](/restaurants/olia) takes Italian foundations and adds a modern twist. The menu changes with the seasons, and there's an emphasis on quality ingredients over heavy sauces. If you're tired of the same old chicken parm, this is a refreshing change.

[Bianco](/restaurants/bianco) keeps things clean and focused. The name means "white" in Italian, and the approach matches — let the ingredients speak, don't over-complicate things. Good pasta, solid execution.

[Panini's Italian Cucina](/restaurants/paninis-italian-cucina-149-street) is the lunch spot. Paninis, pasta, and Italian comfort food at prices that won't empty your wallet. A solid weekday option.

## What to order

- **Fresh pasta** — if a restaurant makes their own pasta, order it. The difference between fresh and dried is night and day. Look for tagliatelle, pappardelle, or stuffed pastas like ravioli and agnolotti.
- **Antipasti** — start with bruschetta, arancini (fried risotto balls), or carpaccio. Italian meals are built for multiple courses.
- **Osso buco** — braised veal shank. When it's on the menu, order it. The meat falls off the bone and the gremolata on top cuts through the richness.
- **Tiramisu** — the Italian dessert. Mascarpone, espresso-soaked ladyfingers, cocoa. If they make it in-house, it's always worth ordering.

## Edmonton's Italian connection

Edmonton has a deep Italian-Canadian community, and it shows in the food. Many of these restaurants are family-run, some for multiple generations. That heritage means the food isn't trend-chasing — it's rooted in tradition, with the kind of consistency that only comes from people who grew up eating this food and care about getting it right.

Book a table, order a bottle of something red, and eat slowly. That's how Italian food is meant to be enjoyed.
