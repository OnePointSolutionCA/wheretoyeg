Finding a good electrician in Edmonton is one of those things you don't think about until a breaker keeps tripping at 11 PM or you need a panel upgrade before the home inspector comes back. The difference between a good electrician and a bad one isn't obvious until something goes wrong — and with electrical work, "wrong" can mean a fire.

## What to look for

- **Licensed and insured.** Non-negotiable. Alberta requires electricians to hold a valid journeyman or master electrician certificate. Ask for the license number and verify it. If they dodge the question, hang up.
- **They pull permits.** Any work beyond swapping a light fixture requires a permit in Edmonton. If an electrician says "we don't need one for this" on a panel upgrade or new circuit, find someone else.
- **Written quotes, not ballpark estimates.** A real electrician will look at the job before quoting. "Probably around $500" on the phone isn't a quote — it's a guess that'll become $900 when they open the wall.
- **They explain what they're doing.** You don't need to understand every wire, but you should know why the job costs what it costs and what they're actually changing.
- **Warranty on work.** Most reputable electricians warranty their labor for at least a year, often two.

## When to call an electrician vs. DIY

**Call an electrician:**
- Breakers trip repeatedly
- Outlets feel warm to the touch
- Lights flicker when appliances turn on
- You smell burning near outlets or the panel
- Any work inside the panel
- Adding new circuits or outlets
- Anything in a bathroom, kitchen, or outdoors (code requirements are stricter)

**Probably fine to DIY:**
- Swapping a light switch (same type, same wiring)
- Replacing a light fixture (if you're comfortable with it)
- Installing a smart thermostat (low voltage)

When in doubt, call. The permit and inspection exist for a reason.

## Older Edmonton homes — what to watch for

If your house was built before 1970, there's a good chance you have aluminum wiring, knob-and-tube wiring, or a panel that's undersized for modern electrical loads. Common issues:

- **60A or 100A panels** that can't handle a modern kitchen, AC unit, and EV charger simultaneously
- **Aluminum wiring** at connections (requires special anti-oxidant compound and compatible devices)
- **Ungrounded outlets** throughout the house (two-prong, no ground wire)
- **Fuse panels** that should have been replaced a decade ago

An electrical inspection before you renovate saves surprises. Most electricians in Edmonton will do one for $150-$300.
