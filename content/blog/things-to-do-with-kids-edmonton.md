Late October in Edmonton is a strange time to be a parent. The leaves are gone, the snow has not really committed yet, and the kids have more energy than the backyard can handle. You need a plan for the next five months, not just for this Saturday.

So here is ours. Everything below is a real place in or around Edmonton that works for kids in the cold months. Where we give prices or hours, we checked them on the official site this week. They change, so tap through before you load everyone into the car.

## Indoor playgrounds for the little ones

When it is minus 20 and your toddler needs to run, an indoor playground is the answer. Most of them have a cafe corner so you can sit with a coffee while the kids climb. Many ask for socks for everyone, so pack a pair for yourself too.

Here are a few spread around the city so you do not have to drive across town:

- **[Hide N Seek Indoor Playground & Cafe](/activities-fun/hide-n-seek-indoor-playground-cafe)** in West Edmonton, handy if you are already near the mall.
- **[PlayDen Kids Cafe](/activities-fun/playden-kids-cafe)** on Airport Road in central Edmonton, close to Blatchford.
- **[U-Play Indoor Playground](/activities-fun/u-play-indoor-playground-in-edmonton)** in the northwest, off 156 Street.
- **[Treehouse Indoor Playground](/activities-fun/treehouse-indoor-playground-north-edmonton)** in North Edmonton, with a [second location in South Edmonton](/activities-fun/treehouse-indoor-playground-south-edmonton).
- **[Bouncy Planet](/activities-fun/bouncy-planet)** and **[Fun4all Indoor Playground](/activities-fun/fun4all-indoor-playground)** on the south side.
- **[Grizzly Cubs Den Indoor Playground And Cafe](/activities-fun/grizzly-cubs-den-indoor-playground-and-cafe)** if you live in Leduc or out by the airport.

A tip from experience: weekday mornings are calm, and Saturday afternoons are packed. If your kid gets overwhelmed by crowds, go early. You can see every option, with hours and maps, on our [indoor playgrounds in Edmonton](/activities-fun/indoor-playground) page.

## Edmonton Valley Zoo

The zoo stays open after summer, just on a shorter schedule. From October 13 to November 22, 2026, the [City of Edmonton](https://www.edmonton.ca/attractions_events/edmonton-valley-zoo) lists hours of 10am to 4pm, Thursday to Sunday and holidays, and closed Monday to Wednesday. Last admission is one hour before closing.

The zoo is home to more than 250 animals. Admission is $8.25 for kids aged 2 to 12, $10.50 for youth, $11.75 for adults, and free for tots under 2. Parking is free right outside the gate. Kids 13 and under need to be with a responsible adult.

Two things to know before you go. First, the zoo is in the middle of a renewal project, so some paths are fenced off and some animals have moved. Second, cold animals are often more active than hot ones, so a crisp fall morning can be a great time to visit. Dress everyone warmly, because most of it is outside.

## Muttart Conservatory

The four glass pyramids in the river valley are a perfect winter escape. Inside, the [Muttart Conservatory](/activities-fun/muttart-conservatory) holds one of the largest indoor plant collections in Canada, with about 700 kinds of plants. Walking from a snowy parking lot into a warm tropical pyramid feels like a tiny vacation, and kids love finding the strange plants.

The city lists admission at $7.75 for kids aged 2 to 12 and free for kids under 2. It is open every day except Christmas Day, and the closest LRT stop is Muttart on the Valley Line. One note: Wednesday evenings from 5 to 9pm are adults only, so plan family visits for the daytime. This year is the Muttart's 50th anniversary, and the city is running a family Halloween afternoon there with face painting, scavenger hunts and crafts.

## Royal Alberta Museum

The [Royal Alberta Museum](/activities-fun/royal-alberta-museum) downtown is one of the best rainy or snowy day plans in the city. Kids 6 and under get in free, youth tickets are $12, and a family ticket is $55 for up to two adults and their kids, to a maximum of eight people, according to the [museum's own page](https://royalalbertamuseum.ca/visit).

Check the days before you go. The museum is closed Mondays and Tuesdays, except holiday Mondays, and stays open until 8pm on Thursdays. The museum also runs free with admission craft nights and kids workshops through the fall, so look at the events calendar when you plan your visit.

## A heads up about TELUS World of Science

A lot of parents still have the science centre at the top of their list. As of this week, the [TELUS World of Science Edmonton](https://telusworldofscienceedmonton.ca/) website says it is temporarily closed. The building was damaged in the July 2026 storms, and news reports in August said the closure would stretch into 2027. Check their site for updates before you promise the kids a visit.

## World Waterpark and Galaxyland

Swimming in November sounds wrong in Edmonton, which is exactly why kids love it. [World Waterpark](/activities-fun/world-waterpark) at West Edmonton Mall has what the mall calls the world's largest indoor wave pool, more than 17 slides and splash zones, and water kept at 31°C all year.

Right next door, [Galaxyland](/activities-fun/galaxyland) is an indoor amusement park with rides sorted into beginner, intermediate and advanced, so little kids and teens can both find something. There is also a Kids Playpark for children 12 and under. It is socks only, and an adult has to stay with the kids the whole time. Some rides have height limits, so a shorter child may need to ride with a paying adult.

A full mall day can get pricey fast. Pick one big attraction per trip, bring snacks if the rules allow it, and save the rest for another weekend.

## Skating, indoors and out

Skating is the most Edmonton thing you can do with kids in winter, and a lot of it is free.

- **Outdoor city rinks and trails.** The [Victoria Park Skating Oval](/activities-fun/victoria-park-skating-oval), the [Victoria IceWay](/activities-fun/victoria-iceway-skating-trail) and the [Rundle Park IceWay](/activities-fun/rundle-park-outdoor-skating-iceway) open once it is cold enough. Opening dates depend on the weather, so watch the city's skating updates in late November and December.
- **Ice Palace at West Edmonton Mall.** [Ice Palace](/activities-fun/ice-palace) has an NHL sized ice surface and public skating sessions all year. It is a good place to practice before the outdoor rinks open.

For little beginners, a helmet is a must, and a short first session is better than a long, cold, tearful one. Our full list of [skating rinks in Edmonton](/activities-fun/skating) has more neighbourhood options.

## Free and cheap ideas

You do not need to spend money every weekend. Some of our favourite family days cost almost nothing.

- **Library programs.** [Edmonton Public Library](https://www.epl.ca/) branches run free programs for kids, from story times for babies to clubs for older kids. Check the events list for your nearest branch.
- **A walk at Elk Island.** [Elk Island National Park](/activities-fun/elk-island-national-park) is less than an hour east of the city. On a sunny fall day it is a great place to look for bison from the car or on a short trail.
- **Neighbourhood parks and sledding hills.** Once the snow comes, every community has a hill. Bundle up, bring a thermos of hot chocolate, and you have a whole afternoon.
