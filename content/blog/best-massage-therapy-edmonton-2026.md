Your back hurts. You sit at a desk all day, or you're on your feet all shift, or you hit the gym too hard and now your traps feel like concrete. Edmonton has no shortage of massage therapists, but finding one who actually fixes the problem instead of rubbing lotion on you for 60 minutes takes some filtering.

## RMTs vs. spa massage — know the difference

**Registered Massage Therapists (RMTs)** are trained to treat injuries, chronic pain, and mobility issues. They're covered by most benefits plans. An RMT appointment feels less like a spa day and more like a targeted repair session.

**Spa massage** is for relaxation. Hot stones, essential oils, dim lights, maybe some harp music. Nothing wrong with it, but if you have a specific injury or chronic tightness, you want an RMT.

Edmonton has both. Here's who does what well.

## Best for deep tissue and injury rehab

**Therapeutic Body Concepts** has two locations (Coronation and High Park) and they're the go-to for people with actual problems. Their therapists dig in — not the "is this pressure okay?" every five minutes kind of deep tissue. They assess, find the knot, and work it out. Benefits-direct billing available.

**Integrated Therapies Massage** takes a clinical approach. If you've got a desk-job neck or post-workout soreness that won't quit, their RMTs build a treatment plan rather than just doing generic full-body sessions each time.

**Coronation Physiotherapy** combines massage with physio — useful if you're rehabbing an injury and need both.

## Best for relaxation and unwinding

**Healing Waters Spa** is the full experience. Hot stone massage, aromatherapy, and they don't talk unless you want them to. The ambiance alone drops your cortisol.

**CeCi Spa** does couples massage and has a proper relaxation lounge. It's where you go for a birthday or anniversary, not Tuesday back pain.

**The Spa at Ambleside** is newer and runs a more boutique feel — smaller, quieter, and they take fewer clients per day so it doesn't feel like a factory.

## Best for Thai massage

**Chiang Mai Thai Massage & Wellness** on Kingsway is the real deal. Traditional Thai massage means no oil, full-body stretching, and pressure point work while you're clothed. It hurts in a productive way. If you've never had Thai massage, start here.

## What to know before booking

- **Check your benefits first.** Most plans cover RMT massage (usually $500-800/year). Spa massage is rarely covered unless the therapist is a registered RMT.
- **Book ahead.** Good RMTs in Edmonton are booked 1-2 weeks out. Same-day availability usually means the therapist is new or the clinic is struggling.
- **Specify what you need.** Don't just say "full body." Tell them where it hurts, what makes it worse, and whether you want relaxation or therapeutic work.
- **Tip is optional at RMT clinics.** Most people tip at spas (15-20%), but tipping at clinical RMT offices isn't expected.

## Hot stone, cupping, and add-ons

Most spas in Edmonton offer hot stone as an upgrade ($15-25 extra). Cupping has gotten popular — those circular bruise marks you see on people at the gym. It works for some people, but ask your therapist if it makes sense for your issue rather than just adding it because it sounds cool.
