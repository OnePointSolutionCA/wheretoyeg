Finding a trustworthy mechanic in Edmonton is like finding a good dentist — once you have one, you never switch. The problem is getting there without paying for unnecessary repairs, inflated labour hours, or parts markups that would make a dealership blush.

Here's how to find a good shop and which ones Edmonton drivers recommend.

## What separates a good shop from a bad one

- **They show you the problem.** A good mechanic walks you to the bay and points at the worn brake pad or the leaking gasket. A bad one just hands you a quote.
- **They quote before they work.** No surprise charges. If they find something extra during the job, they call you before touching it.
- **They don't push unnecessary work.** "Your transmission fluid is fine for another 20,000 km" is something an honest mechanic says. A dishonest one says "while we're in there..."
- **They warranty their work.** Most reputable shops guarantee labour for 12 months or 20,000 km. No warranty means no accountability.

## Dealership vs. independent — when each makes sense

**Go to the dealership when:**
- Your car is under warranty and the work might void it (though this is rarer than dealers claim)
- You need recall or TSB (technical service bulletin) work
- The repair requires dealer-specific diagnostic tools

**Go independent when:**
- Your car is out of warranty
- The work is standard (brakes, oil, tires, suspension)
- You want transparent pricing — independents typically charge $80–120/hour vs. $150–180 at dealers

## Red flags at any shop

- **"You need this done today or it's dangerous."** Unless your brake pads are literally metal-on-metal, most repairs can wait a few days while you get a second opinion.
- **Quoting labour in hours instead of flat rate.** "This is a 4-hour job" can become 6 hours real fast. Flat-rate pricing protects you.
- **Won't let you see the old parts.** If they replaced your brake rotors, you should be able to see the old ones. If they throw them away before you ask, wonder why.
- **Cash-only discounts.** Usually means they're not reporting the income. Not inherently bad for you, but it tells you something about how the business operates.
