## Emergency dentist Edmonton — where to go right now

Dental emergencies don't wait for Monday morning. Here's what counts as an emergency and where to go:

**Go to an emergency dentist if you have:**
- A knocked-out tooth (time-sensitive — within 30 minutes is ideal)
- A cracked or broken tooth with sharp edges
- Severe toothache that won't respond to over-the-counter painkillers
- Swelling in the jaw or face
- A lost filling or crown exposing the tooth

**[Emergency Dental Care](/medical/emergency-dental-care)** is the dedicated emergency clinic. They handle after-hours and same-day emergencies.

Several other clinics also take same-day urgent appointments:
- [Summit Dental](/medical/summit-dental) — call first, they often fit emergencies in
- [Affinity Dental](/medical/affinity-dental---mayfield-common) — walk-in friendly

**If it's a medical emergency** (difficulty breathing, uncontrolled bleeding, facial trauma), go to the ER. Dental clinics handle teeth — hospitals handle everything else.

## Affordable dentist Edmonton — what to know

Alberta doesn't have universal dental coverage, so cost matters. Here's how to keep dental bills manageable:

- **Alberta Works and AISH** cover basic dental for eligible Albertans
- **University of Alberta dental school** offers reduced-rate cleanings and procedures done by supervised students
- **Many clinics** on this list offer direct billing to Alberta Blue Cross, Manulife, Sun Life, and other major plans
- **Ask about payment plans** — most clinics offer them for larger procedures

## How to pick a dentist

- **Check Google reviews** — anything above 4.5 with 100+ reviews is genuinely good
- **Location matters** — you'll skip appointments if it's a 30-minute drive
- **Ask about direct billing** — saves you from paying upfront and waiting for reimbursement
- **First visit is diagnostic** — a good dentist does X-rays and an exam before recommending anything
