Edmonton's Chinese food scene is broad and deep. From weekend dim sum packed with families to fiery Szechuan spots that'll make you sweat, the city has authentic Chinese cooking across multiple regional styles.

## Dim sum

Dim sum is a weekend tradition in Edmonton, and the city has proper cart-service and menu-order spots.

[Dim Sum Delight Chinese Cuisine](/restaurants/dim-sum-delight-chinese-cuisine) is a reliable go-to. Har gow (shrimp dumplings), siu mai, cheung fun (rice noodle rolls), and egg tarts — all the classics done well. Go early on weekends because it fills up.

[Sunning Family Restaurant](/restaurants/sunning-family-restaurant-dim-sum-chinese-food) (新宁) is the kind of place where Chinese families actually eat. That's always a good sign. Their dim sum selection is extensive, and the congee is worth ordering alongside your dumplings.

## Szechuan and spice

[Szechuan Castle](/restaurants/szechuan-castle) is Edmonton's go-to for the mala (numbing-spicy) flavors of Sichuan province. Mapo tofu with real Szechuan peppercorns, dan dan noodles, and dry-fried green beans that'll ruin you for any other version. If you like heat with complexity, this is your spot.

## Cantonese and classic Chinese-Canadian

[Emperor's Palace Chinese Cuisine](/restaurants/emperors-palace-chinese-cuisine) is a classic banquet-style Chinese restaurant. It's the kind of place you go for a big family dinner — lazy susan spinning, ten courses, someone's birthday. Their seafood dishes are strong, and the Peking duck is worth ordering ahead.

[The Lingnan](/restaurants/the-lingnan) is a staple. Cantonese comfort food, generous portions, and the kind of old-school Chinese restaurant vibe that's getting harder to find. Chow mein, fried rice, and sweet and sour pork done the way you remember it.

[Golden Rice Bowl](/restaurants/golden-rice-bowl) serves reliable Chinese-Canadian dishes alongside more traditional Cantonese cooking. It's a neighborhood spot that keeps regulars coming back week after week.

## Worth the trip

[Shanghai 456](/restaurants/shanghai-456) brings Shanghainese flavors — xiao long bao (soup dumplings), lion's head meatballs, and braised pork belly. Shanghai cuisine is sweeter and more delicate than Cantonese or Szechuan, and this spot does it justice.

[Himalayan Village](/restaurants/himalayan-village-mill-woods-town-centre-edmonton) in Mill Woods blends Chinese and South Asian influences. It's a unique crossover that reflects Edmonton's multicultural food scene.

## What to know before you go

- **Dim sum timing** — go between 10 AM and noon on weekends for the best selection. By 1 PM the popular dishes start running out.
- **Family style** — most Chinese restaurants are built for sharing. Order one dish per person plus one extra, put everything in the middle, and pass it around.
- **Tea service** — at dim sum, your tea is refilled for free. Flip the teapot lid open when you need more — that's the signal to the server.
- **Cash vs. card** — some of the older spots still prefer cash. Check before you sit down.
- **MSG** — many traditional Chinese restaurants use MSG. It's a flavor enhancer, not something to fear. If you have a sensitivity, ask, but don't assume it's bad.

Edmonton's Chinese food is the real thing. Skip the food court versions and eat where the families eat.
