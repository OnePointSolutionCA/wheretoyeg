Finding a hair salon in Edmonton takes five minutes. Finding a stylist who listens, doesn't fry your hair with bleach, and charges a price that matches the result — that takes research.

Edmonton has over 400 salons. Most are fine. A handful are exceptional. Here's how to tell the difference.

## What to look for in a salon

The salon itself matters less than the individual stylist. Two chairs apart in the same salon can be completely different experiences. When you're shopping:

- **Check their actual work, not the salon's feed.** Most salon Instagrams mix work from every stylist. Find the specific person's portfolio.
- **Ask about their process for colour.** A good colourist does a consultation before booking the appointment, not while you're in the chair with foils in.
- **Watch how they handle pricing.** Vague pricing ("it depends") usually means a surprise at the till. Good salons quote a range upfront.

## Best for colour and balayage

**Blonde Brunette Hair Studio** specializes in colour — it's in the name. Their balayage work is consistently natural-looking, which is harder than it sounds. They're one of the few salons in Edmonton where the "lived-in blonde" actually looks lived-in, not stripey.

**Dirty Blonde Studio** is another colour-focused spot. They handle colour corrections — if someone else wrecked your hair and you need it fixed, this is where you go. Not cheap, but you're paying to undo damage.

**C-Blonde Specialist** runs a smaller operation, which means more attention per client. If you want platinum or ashy tones that don't turn brassy in two weeks, book here.

## Best for cuts and styling

**Frenchies Hair** does sharp, modern cuts. They're good with textured hair and don't default to the same layered bob on everyone. Walk-ins available, which is rare for a salon this good.

**Chrome Spa Salon** combines cut quality with a spa-like environment. If you want the full experience — wash, cut, style, and not feeling rushed — this is the spot.

**The Hair Loft YEG** is where you go for editorial-style cuts. The stylists here stay current with trends without pushing anything you didn't ask for.

**Carv Hair Team** and **Volume Salons** are solid all-rounders. Consistent cuts, reasonable pricing, and they don't oversell products at checkout.

## Best for textured and curly hair

Not every stylist in Edmonton knows how to cut curly hair. If you have curls, coils, or natural texture, find someone trained in dry cutting or the DevaCut method. Ask specifically — "do you cut curly hair?" is different from "can you cut curly hair?"

**Hala Salon** works with a range of textures and has stylists experienced with Middle Eastern and South Asian hair types.

## What to expect to pay

Edmonton hair salon pricing in 2026:

- **Women's cut and style:** $55–120
- **Men's cut:** $35–60
- **Full balayage:** $180–350
- **Full colour (single process):** $120–200
- **Colour correction:** $200–500+ depending on damage

Prices climb with stylist experience and salon location. Downtown and Whyte Ave trend higher. Suburban salons run 20–30% less for comparable work.

## Tips for a good experience

- **Bring reference photos.** Not celebrity photos — photos of actual hair colour and cuts on real people. Pinterest is better than Google Images for this.
- **Be honest about maintenance.** If you wash your hair twice a week and never use heat tools, don't ask for a style that requires daily blow-drying.
- **Tip 15–20%.** Standard in Edmonton for salon services.
